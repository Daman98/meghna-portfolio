Org Reporting

Designing a centralized organization-level reporting experience to help Learning and Skill Development admins track learning adoption, engagement, completion, skills, and certification outcomes across AI Skill Navigator.

User scenario

The data exists.
The organisational story doesn’t.

Current system

  1. Playlist-level visibility Insights are primarily centered around individual playlists rather than overall organizational learning.
  2. No centralized organizational view Admins lack one place to understand overall AI Skill adoption and engagement.
  3. Limited learner visibility Understanding how learners are progressing across learning experiences is difficult.
  4. Limited skill visibility Admins cannot easily connect learning activity with the skills being developed.
  5. Certification outcomes are disconnected Certification information is not easily connected to the broader learning story.
  6. Difficult to answer leadership questions Admins have to piece together information before understanding overall program performance.

Design opportunity

01

Design direction

User testing

I conducted remote usability testing through the UserTesting platform to evaluate how effectively the reporting experience communicated complex learning data to L&D administrators.

Participants completed two realistic scenarios using interactive prototypes while thinking aloud. I used an A/B testing approach to compare two design directions and understand which version offered clearer information hierarchy, stronger data comprehension, and more intuitive interactions.

01

What I Evaluated

  • Clarity and usefulness of the reporting metrics
  • Ability to identify important insights quickly
  • Comprehension of charts, labels, and terminology
  • Discoverability of key actions
  • Expectations around the completion interaction
  • Understanding of empty states and messages
  • Overall preference between Design A and Design B
02

Research Approach

Participants explored both concepts, completed the assigned tasks, and explained what they expected to happen at each stage. I observed their behavior, captured areas of hesitation or confusion, and followed up with questions to understand the reasoning behind their choices.

03

Key Findings

Testing revealed which design made the reporting story easier to scan and understand. Participants responded more positively to the direction with a clearer hierarchy, stronger grouping of related information, and more visible primary actions.

The sessions also uncovered opportunities to simplify terminology, strengthen interaction cues, and make empty-state messaging more useful and actionable.

04

Design Outcome

Based on the findings, I selected the stronger design direction and refined the experience by:

  • Improving the hierarchy of key metrics
  • Clarifying labels and supporting copy
  • Increasing the visibility of important actions
  • Refining the completion interaction
  • Making empty states clearer and more informative
  • Reducing the effort required to interpret reporting insights
